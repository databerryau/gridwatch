## The verdict

1. **The engine is excellent and nobody has played the game.** Both were true on 14 October. Since then seven work
   packages have shipped and no person has been asked.
2. **The game is not ready for the playtest it is waiting for.** A held S or X key stops whole stations, two
   buttons cannot be clicked with a mouse, the how-to describes a button that is not there, and a click on a knob
   changes its value. A tester will meet one of these inside six minutes.
3. **The evidence the plan leans on is weaker than the spec says.** The "newcomer" script has no reaction time.
   With one, it drops a letter on two of three dailies (§1.2). Par wastes $0.3M to $1M on most days, which is why
   scripts beat it.
4. **The strategy has two holes that building cannot fill:** the share card lands on a phone and the game needs a
   desk, and there is no way to count a single visit.
5. **Nothing stops a broken build going live**, and one has.

## Part 1. Strategy

### 1.1 Nobody has played, and the plan's own checkpoints were skipped

The repo has 670 commits, about 1.8 MB of game code, 2.4 MB of tests and 1.6 MB of spec and records. One person
has played it. The plan had three checks with people in it (W2 with two strangers, gate 1, gate 2). None has run.
W3, W4, W6, W7, W8, W9 and W10 were each built "ahead of" a playtest, and the gate 1 kit has been ready since
22 October. Everything decided since 1 October rests on the owner's own play and on scripts. Past sessions of mine
went along with each step; the owner raised it on 22 October before I did.

Q-131 (no new content until gate 1) is the right rule. The risk now is the opposite one: three weeks have passed
and the only work done was another round of tuning against a script (W11).

### 1.2 The scripts flatter the game

Every claim about a newcomer ("B or better on 57 of 59 dailies", "about 295 real seconds") comes from
`tests/lib/play.js` `follow()`. That script presses in the same tick an ask appears and sends the input straight to
the sim. It skips reading the line, finding the control, the two presses on a START, the hold on D and E, and the
six-click air-con booking. A person does all of that while the clock runs at up to 150 grid seconds a second.

*Measured once for this review* (the same loop as `follow()`, with a reaction time before each press; three dailies):


| Daily | 0 s (the spec's script) | 3 s | 6 s | 9 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 Nov, heatwave | 1008 A | 967 B | 800 B | 887 B |
| 17 Nov, low wind | 994 A | 997 A | 1009 A | 1005 A |
| 18 Nov, storm | 990 A | 947 B | 959 B | 957 B |

Every light stayed on in all twelve runs, which is good news for gate 1's "finish" line. The letter is what
moves: a person-paced follower drops from A to B on two of the three days, and on the heatwave day ends $0.5M to
over $3M behind par. At zero delay the script reproduces `follow()` exactly (35 presses, 1008). The script lives in
this session's scratch folder, not the repo; if the number is wanted in the spec, the delay belongs in `follow()`.
Three dailies is a small sample.

### 1.3 A first shift is a reading test at speed

On today's daily (a heatwave Friday) the coach gave 35 asks averaging 26 words. Nineteen of them came between 17:27
and 19:50, which is 57 real seconds at the peak's 150×. That is about 500 words a minute before a single press.
Some of those asks undo each other within seconds:

- 19:49:40 "Raise the battery GUARD to 200 MW", 19:49:42 "Set it to idle", 19:49:45 "Raise the battery GUARD to 400 MW".
- 17:43:05 "turn its GUARD down to 0 MW, then discharge it", after a day of being told the GUARD catches the fall.
- 19:06:52 "Start CCGT 1 now: it reaches the grid at 19:55, too late for 19:10."
- Six separate air-con bookings, one per suburb, in 13 grid seconds (known, still open from W11).
- Demand response called three times (17:48, 18:48, 19:49), each as a new ask.

The player can pause and act while paused, so a careful newcomer will pause at every ask. Then the day is not six
minutes and the "running clock, no RUN button" pillar becomes pause, read, press. The north star says "the plan
comes together". On day one the plan is the coach's.

### 1.4 The real game is tested by nothing

From the second shift the coach is off and the line states the problem. That is the game people would come back
to. No script can play it (the line names no action), gate 1 covers only a first shift, and gate 2 asks only whether
they came back. Its difficulty, its fairness and whether it is fun are unmeasured even by a machine.

### 1.5 The share loop breaks at the tap

A share card arrives on a phone. The game needs a desktop window of 1280 by 600. The gate 2 kit already notes that a
1080p laptop at 150% scaling (most 13 to 14 inch work laptops) is under 600 px high and gets the "window too small"
page first. The launch venues in §11 (Show HN, LinkedIn, WattClarity) are desk readers and energy people. That is
the audience this game can reach, and the realism ledger is what would win them. OD-1 still says "the curious
public", and gate 1 allows at most one energy professional.

### 1.6 There is no way to count anything

Telemetry has waited on a Cloudflare account since 20 October. OD-1's success test ("strangers finish a session
and share it") has no number and no date. A launch post with no counter is one spike that teaches nothing.

### 1.7 The top of the score rewards luck and knowing the day generator

Q-130's own numbers say the lean script beats par on about 45% of days by skipping security that was not tested
that day. MARG'S CHAIR needs 4% under par, so the top rank goes to whoever carried less spare and was not caught.
The day generator's rules are fixed and learnable: no trip before 09:00 or after 21:00, at most two, the evening
one announced. A returning player can drop the spare the minute the second trip lands. Carbon is 36 to 53% of the
total and barely moves, so an A means "within about 2%" of a number that is half fixed.

The code review found the cause underneath (§2.3, §2.9). Par calls demand response it does not need after most
evening trips, at $490,000 a call, and leaves a quarter to a half of the day's water unused. On the stored ladder
a scripted player beats par on 318 of the 446 dailies. Q-128 and Q-130 treated this as a question of where to
draw the letter lines. The benchmark is what is soft.

### 1.8 Too much surface for six minutes

Today's end card is 492 words; D-25 says the verdict reads in 45 seconds. The desk shows about 55 enabled controls
at 04:30. Every step since W1 added surface and none removed any.

### 1.9 The documents

`SPEC.md` is 187 KB. Its §3 is frozen at a build eleven steps old, its §4 "target" is now built, and the truth is
spread over eleven step records. §10 says 916 tests in 45 to 75 s; today it is 1,197 in 98 s here and 8 to 9 minutes
in CI. Tests parse the spec's headings. Each new session pays to read it, and the owner cannot.

### 1.10 What I would do, in order

1. **This week: two people, 15 minutes each, on a call with their screen shared.** That is the W2 that was skipped.
   Two real sessions now are worth more than five someday.
2. **Before that, the owner plays three dailies with the coach off** and writes down the letter, stress and confusion.
3. **Fix the Part 2 defects a tester would hit.**
4. **Give the follower a reaction time** and quote that number in the spec.
5. **Name the audience.** My call: energy-curious people at a desk, reached through the owner's own network first.
   Lead the launch with the real-incident days (`?date=`), which make a better headline than "Daily #33".
6. **A visit counter before any public post.**
7. **Keep the freeze.** After gate 1, cut before adding.

## Part 3. What the code review says, taken together

1. **The engine is the asset.** Three reviewers tried to break determinism, the information barrier and the
   two-sims case with scripts and could not. The stored par data is exact.
2. **The keyboard is the liability.** K1 to K5, S1, G4, G5 and K7 are one family: key repeat, lost keyups, two key
   maps and clicks that act. A tester will hit at least one in a 6-minute session.
3. **The tests could not see any of it.** The fake DOM sends no auto-repeat, has no CSS and no layout, so K1, V1
   and S7 pass 1,197 tests. The suite protects the sim and pins the page's sentences.
4. **The numbers behind three decisions are not what their sentences say:** the coach-follower's 57 of 59 (X2),
   the chair's 13% (X4) and the letter calibration (X3).
5. **Par is honest and soft** (P1, P2). That, more than letter lines, is why scripts beat it.
6. **W11 this morning added two coach faults** (C1, C7) and made a third worse (C2). It was tuned against a
   script that cannot see them.
7. **The release path has no gate** and has already let a red build and an unbumped build go live (X1, R3).
8. **About a tenth of the repo is dead:** the bench and its chart code, the v2 tools and their goldens, tests of
   the frozen legacy page, four params, the coal-stop rule, `game.refusal`, HOT WATER HOLD's text.
