## 1. The premises, one by one

### 1.1 "The daily is the first session" (OD-11, O-1)
**Earning its keep: half.** The seeded day gives fairness (D-9) and the shared-seed share card (Phase 4), both good. But the *first* session decides whether a stranger finishes, and today it is whichever date they arrive on: a tie-export trip at noon on 20261014 (a rise, a non-event), a heatwave on 20261016 (the line breaks glass; B), a cloud front on the weekend. Q-65 already says "2e can pin the first shift (§4.1) as a scenario variant by data alone" (desk/README.md:3554). I would go further: **the first shift should be the same designed seed for everyone, forever**, with a trip that falls, a guard the player set, and one feeder, not two; it is the game's demo level. The calendar daily takes over from day 2 (O-1's "I've done this before" switch, not yet built). Cost S (data + a storage flag). Realism unaffected.

### 1.2 Five minutes (OD-5, D-2)
**Earning its keep, but the minutes are not evenly fun.** 288–307 s measured. Pre-dawn (10 s) and the belly (57 s) are nearly empty on a competent day; the peak (84 s) is empty *by design* when the plan is good (pillar 2: "the annunciator stays dark"). Q-90 measured the median day's worst gap at 36 real s and left it to playtest (README:3579). The problem is not length; it is that the two most dramatic stretches (peak and night) have nothing to do when you have done well. See §3.6.

### 1.3 No progression, no money (OD-16, W-5)
**Spec-true, screen-false.** There is no shop, but the end card is a $ table (SUPPLY $4.03M, OUTAGES $53, CARBON $4.44M, ALL-IN $8.47M, "$4.46 per household") and the briefing opens with the price list. That reads as money. Meanwhile the progression the spec *does* have, cover plates handing over one control per daily (O-3, dailies 2–5) and the streak (Y-3), is unbuilt. Today's "one more go" pull is a PLAY THIS DAY AGAIN button. The W-5 guard rail is right (no perks), but **progression of understanding is not an economy**: concept cards (O-4), cover plates (O-3) and a visible "controls handed over 3/8" are the cheap pull. Cost: 2e.

### 1.4 Par as the yardstick (OD-10, S-4, Q-48)
**Earning its keep as a fairness gate; not as a face, and not as a skill curve.** Par is honest (information barrier, re-plans like the player). But the player never sees par play: the score is `1004 (par 1000)` against an invisible opponent. And the letters barely separate skill: on 20 `desk` days the competent proxy gets A on 17 and the lean proxy on 15 (median points 1004 vs 985; `agency-par-1-20.txt`), and SPEC's own S-12 table says lean-proxy A ≤ 40% is **"not met"** at 48% (SPEC.md:859–860) because the 10-MWh promise won (Q-48). The ceiling is low on purpose, fine for strangers; but it leaves nothing for day 5. Make par visible as a teacher (D-21's "your headroom, inertia and battery charge against par's at the same minute"; D-22's "par's decisions you didn't make"): cost 2d.

### 1.5 AGC on by default (OD-7)
**Earning its keep.** The owner's v2 verdict ("actions faster than a human can make them") settles it; HAND exists for the harder mode (Q-64, halved speeds, `HAND-FLOWN` tag). Keep.

### 1.6 "Desk as instrument" with motorised levers (OD-14, K-1, L-6)
**Not earning its keep as built.** K-1's accept asks that ≥50% of dispatch inputs be made on desk controls (SPEC.md:943). The follower's 24 inputs on 20261014 were 10 START/STOP guards, 6 soak bookings (suburb card), 2 restores (Enter at a card), 3 battery orders, 1 GUARD, 2 commits (`follow-20261014.txt`:7–30) and **zero lever, wheel or knob moves**. The objective line never asks for one (`grep -c drag app/objective.js` → 0; "lever" appears only in the held-levers warning, objective.js:401–407). **[corrected]** Par's inputs are `start/stop/guard/battery/tie/flex/restore/callDR/armRERT/standDownRERT` and `planLoad` (sim/autopilot.js:748), and `planLoad` is the whole-plan re-dispatch (sim/step.js:254; app/system.js:12), not a lever move. And a lever the *player* moves is a `basePoint` edit that sets `sys.edited` and holds every lever until RE-DISPATCH (app/system.js:66–98; Q-18 "A lever moved by hand holds the levers until RE-DISPATCH"), which is the opposite of L-6's "AGC mode: moving a lever writes that unit's next arrive-by keyframe … There is no MANUAL state" (SPEC.md:1266–1270). So the five motorised faders, pillar 1's centrepiece, are a display the player watches and a trap if touched; the controls that matter are a two-click button, a ring and a dial. Honest to the NEM (NEMDE moves plant), but the spec should say so and the UI budget should go to the three controls the player really uses. Two ways out, §4 item 7.

### 1.7 The ban on *Grid Operator*'s plan → RUN → cards (OD-16, §7, W-5)
**The ban is on the words, not the loop.** SPEC.md:2148: "Apart from first-shift coaching, the respond card (K-16) is the only thing that holds the clock, and it offers no choices." Today the clock is held by the respond card (K-16), the alarm panel (Q-46), and two feeder cards a day with RESTORE and GO ON buttons (Q-91, README:4555–4575). The tray carries market notices with a button, the suburb card has BOOK/CANCEL, and the objective line *is* a decision card with the decision pre-written. The game has converged on cards because cards are how a stranger reads a control room. The real differentiation (and it is real) is the physics you watch and the desk that reacts, not the absence of cards. Retire the §7 sentence (already false twice) and judge each hold on whether it is a decision worth holding for. The feeder card is not (§3.4); a "the price just spiked: sell or hold the guard?" card at the peak would be.

### 1.8 The Q-91 holds
**Right mechanism, wrong moment.** Holding the clock for a decision is the game's best tool against the reflex test (OD-8). Spending two holds a day on "press Enter to close a repaired feeder", on 85% of days, in the first 10–25 real seconds of the morning ramp, before the player has done anything else, is the game's most prominent chore. And the taught action is immediately punished: the cold-load dip lights UNDER FREQ and `BAND LOW 5 MIN` (49.767 Hz at 06:55:02, `tl-20261014.txt`:48–50). Keep the hold; move it (§3.4).

### 1.9 Realism sacred (OD-6)
**It has paid where it created a decision (the GUARD ring, Q-1) and cost where it created a number.** Three places where realism hurts fun and the owner has now opened the door:
- VCR $30,000/MWh (Q-48) makes a 10-minute district outage worth more than the whole day's fuel: the no-input day ends `OUTAGES $147.99M … Each SAIDI-minute today cost customers about $3.07M at VCR` (`tl-20261014.txt`:510). The score collapses to "did you shed".
- RERT at $16,000/MWh (Q-2) plus a line willing to call it: on the heatwave the obedient player gets a B. A rare, dramatic break-glass is right; a hint that breaks it for you is not.
- Cold-load pickup ×1.5 (K-13) makes every taught restore an alarm.
None of these need the physics bent. They need the *presentation* of consequences chosen for the player, and the line to stop pre-deciding.

## 3. Findings (grounded, strongest first)

### 3.1 The signature moment plays at the wrong speed — design-weakness, HIGH
K-15: the full 30-s watch "plays the first time ever, and on anything new" (SPEC.md:1095); director.js:101 `d.version = !d.seen.watch || newRocof ? 'full' : 'compact'`; Q-43: Esc skips from the next trip. The first contingency of the day is Act II's on roughly 60% of non-heat days (Q-67: unit 25% / smelter 20% / tie 15% / front 40%, README:3556). So the once-only slow motion is spent on a belly event: on 20261014 a tie *export* trip whose captions are "Load is lost … Frequency rises" and whose card says `PEAK 50.196 Hz: contained ✓` (`tl-20261014.txt`:141–151); on 20261015 a unit trip at 13:45 contained at 49.695 Hz with 172 MW from a battery the player had not yet set (`tl-20261015.txt`:155–166). The evening trip, the one §4.1 and D-21 are built around and the one the player's GUARD catches, then plays at ×0.5 (`competent-20261014.txt`:88–134). The "YOUR GUARD: 400 MW" caption (watch.js:43) flashes by in about 3 real s.
**Proposal (S, presentation only):** the full watch plays for the first *falling* trip ≥ 300 MW (or the first Act III contingency) and on anything new; belly events play compact unless they shed. Keep the once-only rule per *kind* (rise vs fall). Let the watch's battery beat name the player: "YOUR GUARD caught 418 of 620 MW". Realism: none affected.

### 3.2 The live link has no ending — design-weakness, HIGH (known gap, but it is what strangers meet now)
app/endcard.js:72–96 and 100–150: `Day over: 04:00.` → `SCORE n · letter ★` (browser) → a dollar table with "× $30,000 (VCR)" → SAIDI → seed/hash → DOWNLOAD REPLAY LOG · PLAY THIS DAY AGAIN. No moment, no lowest-Hz line, no closing line, no share, no history. Pillar 5 has no surface, and Gate 1's "≥3/5 explain their grade", "retell the moment" cannot be met by this screen. The owner is playtesting on this build.
**Proposal (S–M, a 2d-lite before 2d):** one screen in this order: the moment (time, lowest Hz, what caught it with the player's GUARD MW; D-21's first bullet only), three plain marks vs par (dark MWh · c/kWh · t/MWh, ★ at par or better; D-20), the Y-7 closing line, a COPY SHARE TEXT button (Y-4 is specified to the character), then the $ table behind "?". The counterfactual (D-22) and the real-incident card (D-24) can wait.

### 3.3 Follow-the-line is the whole game — design-weakness, HIGH
Q-18/Q-28 made the objective line say *exactly* what to press ("Start CCGT 2 now", "Stop it", "Charge the battery at 70 MW", "Raise the battery GUARD to 400 MW"), while O-3 says "Marg never states the optimal plan" (SPEC.md:1611). The follower scores 1004 with 24 actions (`follow-20261014.txt`:4–30); the lean proxy gets A on 15 of 20 days against the competent proxy's 17 (`agency-par-1-20.txt`); SPEC's own S-12 row says lean ≤ 40% A is "not met" (SPEC.md:859). Nothing asks the player to read the Live Stack and decide: the line never asks for a drag or a lever (`grep -c drag app/objective.js` → 0), par never drags (§1.6), and L-4's drag (render/livestack.js `commit()`) is reachable only by a player who invents it. The skill ceiling above "obey" is the 4 points between 1004 and 1000. "The plan comes together" is true only in the sense that the line's plan comes together.
**Proposal (S for the words, M for the stack):** after the first shift, or behind an always-visible COACH ON/OFF (O-1's "I've done this before"), the line states the *forecast* ("150 MW short from 06:25; the tie is limited until 11:15") and lights the L-9 glow set; it names a unit only in the ACT NOW kind, when the latest start is within reach. A "no coach" tag on the share card (beside Y-4's "hand-flown"). Realism: operators get LOR notices, not orders.

### 3.4 The morning ramp is a chore queue: two feeder cards and six soak bookings before anything has happened — design-weakness, MEDIUM-HIGH (challenges Q-68 and Q-91)
All four dailies: FEEDER-CARD twice, at 06:55/07:15, 07:15/07:40, 06:50/07:25, 07:00/07:50. Each holds the clock (`HELD 0× · R restore · Esc on`) for a card whose only sensible answer is Enter (GO ON leaves the feeder dark *and yours*, so nobody chooses it). It is the first thing a stranger is held for, 10–25 real seconds into the day, and the reward is an UNDER FREQ tile and `BAND LOW 5 MIN` (`tl-20261014.txt`:44–50, :72–74). Q-91's rationale ("the hand-back becomes a decision") does not hold: there is no decision. In the same fifteen grid-minutes the line asks for six soak bookings, one suburb at a time, for 20–140 MW each (`follow-20261014.txt` 06:46–07:00), with no city-wide control. Q-68 wanted RESTORE practised by players who never shed, fair; but two a day plus six bookings, every day, in the stretch where the line already has the player starting units, is drudgery.
**Proposal (S):** (a) one feeder per day; on the first shift its hand-back is the *taught* restore (§4.1 already says one clearance on the forced first shift); (b) no UNDER FREQ chime for 5 min after a permissive-approved restore (keep the tile); (c) the second feeder, when there is one, lands in Act II so restoring competes with the belly decision; (d) one "SOAK THE CITY" booking (or BOOK ALL on the suburb card) that books every suburb's soak at once, with the per-suburb card kept for the air-con cycle; (e) keep Q-91's hold. Realism: DNSP hand-backs are real; a retailer's ripple-control soak is sent to the whole fleet of tanks, not negotiated suburb by suburb.

### 3.5 The briefing is 248 words and opens with the price list — design-weakness / spec-stale, MEDIUM
D-3: ≤60 words, ≤20 s (SPEC.md:1407). next.html:204 (48 words), :206 (103 words), shell.js:116–118 (57 words), plus `dayText`, `watchFor` and the seed. O-2 wants the first real action within 60 s of the first click. 2e owns the briefing, but the live link today puts a wall of text between the link and the desk, and the first sentence a stranger reads about the goal is "$30,000/MWh for each MWh dark that is yours".
**Proposal (S):** goal line + day line + Watch for + one how-to sentence ("The line above the desk says what is needed. To start a unit, click its ○ twice."); the score formula and the keys behind "?". Measure with the existing word count.

### 3.6 The peak has nothing to do when the plan is good — design-weakness, MEDIUM (challenges pillar 2's "the annunciator stays dark")
`competent-20261014.txt`:80, 18:32: `✓ STEADY Enough plant is committed for the rest of the day. Highest demand ahead: about 6,600 MW at 19:30`, N-1 TIGHT, and 84 real seconds of PEAK at 150× in which the only event is the trip. The pillar wants the climax to be the absence of alarms; a stranger experiences it as the game going quiet when the music should swell. The real decision the peak holds, sell the battery at the price peak or keep it as guard (Q-1's whole point), is pre-decided by the line (`Gas is setting the price ($74). Discharge the battery at 100 MW, all the GUARD leaves free, down to 20%`). **[corrected]** The spot price is drawn in the Live Stack's header (render/livestack.js:600–611, `priceText(pm) + '/MWh'`) and quoted in the line's own text; it is not among the header chips (LIGHTS ON / COST / CO₂ / ALL-IN) and nothing on the battery dial says what the next hour of discharge is worth against the guard's trip cover.
**Proposal (S–M):** the line *describes* at the peak ("Gas is setting the price: $132. The battery is full. Your GUARD covers 400 MW of a 620 MW trip.") and stops ordering the MW; the dial's K-10 preview shows "next hour ≈ $n" against "guard covers n MW". Realism: that is what a battery trader and a control-room operator argue about every evening.

### 3.7 The line sends obedient players to a B on the hardest days — design-weakness, MEDIUM
`follow-20261016.txt`:4 (heatwave): points **927** (B), RERT $4,081k, DR $980k, ALL-IN $15,907k; par's ALL-IN $14,749k with no RERT. Realism: AEMO contracts RERT days ahead; a desk breaking glass at 18:51 for 19:10 is already the game's abstraction.
