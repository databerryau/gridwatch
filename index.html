SHIFT BRIEFING — 04:00 → 04:00

You are the control-room dispatcher for a 7.7 GW regional power system. Your one physical law: generation must equal demand at every instant, or system frequency drifts off 50.000 Hz. Too far, too long, and the grid goes black.

  • Hold frequency inside 49.85 – 50.15 Hz. Penalties accrue outside the band.
  • Click any building on the map to open its controls. Machines ramp at realistic rates — coal is a freight train, batteries are instant.
  • Commit units early. Coal takes 2 h to start, the CCGT 45 min. The evening peak will not wait for you.
  • Watch the duck curve: solar dies at sunset exactly as demand peaks. Plan the ramp.
  • Storms, heatwaves, unit trips and cloud fronts are on today's menu. The log gives warnings — read it.
  • You are paid spot price for energy served. Shedding load costs $16,600/MWh. Blackout ends your career.

Shift runs 24 h in ≈12 min. Space pauses — planning while paused is allowed, and wise. Open MANUAL any time for grid-physics background.

OPERATIONS COACH

OPERATOR MANUAL

FREQUENCY = BALANCE

All generators spin in lockstep at 50 Hz. Surplus generation accelerates them (frequency rises); deficit slows them (frequency falls). Frequency is the grid's live balance meter — steer it by matching supply to demand.

THE MAP

Every asset is a building: click it (or its tile in the FLEET strip) to open controls in the dock. Bars above each plant show output vs available capacity. Smoke, spinning blades, dam level, battery charge and city lights all reflect the live simulation — a dark district means load shedding.

INERTIA

Heavy spinning machines (coal, gas, hydro) resist frequency change like a flywheel. With few synchronous units online — e.g. a solar-heavy midday — the same MW imbalance moves frequency far faster. Watch the INERTIA chip: below ~1,600 the grid is twitchy.

GOVERNOR / FCAS

Online units automatically inject or withdraw a little power against frequency deviation (droop response), and the battery contributes fast frequency response. This buys you seconds, not minutes — it saturates, silently drains battery charge, and you must still redispatch to restore balance.

RAMP RATES & COMMITMENT

Setpoints aren't teleports. Coal moves ~12 MW/min, the CCGT 35, hydro 130, peakers 160, battery instantly. Off units need startup time (coal 2 h, CCGT 45 min, peakers 8 min). Running a unit above ~96% of capacity for long raises its trip risk.

THE DUCK CURVE

Solar suppresses net demand at midday, then vanishes into the evening peak. The dashed cyan forecast shows renewables ahead; NET LOAD RAMP +1H warns how much dispatchable power you must add per hour. The 17:00–20:00 ramp is the hardest part of the shift.

BATTERY

500 MW / 1,000 MWh. Negative setpoint charges (you buy energy at spot), positive discharges. Two hours at full power and it's empty — save charge for the peak and for trip response.

HYDRO

Fast and cheap but energy-limited: today's water allocation is 7,500 MWh (~8 h at full output) — the reservoir behind the dam drains visibly. Ration it for peaks and emergencies.

INTERCONNECTOR

Imports up to 800 MW at a flat $58/MWh contract (export pays $40). Cheap firm supply — until a storm trips the line.

UFLS — LOAD SHEDDING

If frequency falls to 49.2 / 49.0 / 48.8 Hz, under-frequency relays automatically disconnect blocks of customers to save the system — you'll see districts go dark. Unserved energy is valued at $16,600/MWh. Load restores in stages once frequency holds ≥49.95.

SCARCITY PRICING

Spot price rises steeply as reserve margin thins, up to the $16,600 cap during shedding. Your load is hedged with $300-strike cap contracts, so you're paid at most $300/MWh for energy served — price spikes never print money. They set what you pay for battery charging, and they're the grid screaming that you're one trip from disaster.

CURTAILMENT

Wind/solar LIMIT sliders cap renewable output. Use them if over-frequency threatens (e.g. sudden loss of load) or to keep thermal units above their minimums.

EMERGENCY TOOLS

Click the CITY for load-side tools. DEMAND RESPONSE: 3 calls, each drops 350 MW for 1 h at $1,400/MWh. RESERVE DIESEL: 300 MW, 20 min start, $2,600/MWh. Expensive — losing the grid is more expensive.

SCORING

Graded on session P&L, % of time in the normal band, and unserved energy. CO₂ is tracked for the debrief. Blackout = automatic F.
