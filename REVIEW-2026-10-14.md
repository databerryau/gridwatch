> Since 2026-10-15 the SPEC was rewritten (§9.1 Q-104). Every `SPEC.md:NNNN` line reference in this review and its reports points into `SPEC-history.md`, which keeps the old line numbers.

Build reviewed: `main` at 52da14a (v4-core-2c.1), the live game. Read-only; nothing in the repo was changed.
The owner's framing for this review: "do not take anything in the spec as gospel, everything is up for grabs to make the best game."

How it was made: ten reader agents, each with one lens (a stranger's first five minutes; fun and game design; what the player actually decides; the trip moment and the clock holds; realism and what the player learns; code health; scoring, the daily and sharing; the desk and map; whether the spec is true and what the scope is; the genre and missing hooks). The completeness critic and the automatic synthesis were cut by a usage limit, so this synthesis is mine. Nothing was checked in a browser; pixel claims are arithmetic from the CSS and are marked.

Section 8 is the interview: 15 decisions for the owner, each with options and a recommendation, ordered by how much the answer changes the game.

## 1. The verdict in ten lines

1. The engine is excellent: deterministic, replayable, sourced against the NEM, labelled where it simplifies. The suite is green (916 tests). Keep all of it.
2. The day works. A stranger who does what the plan line says gets an A with every light on (975 to 1046 points on the two dailies played); a stranger who does nothing gets a clear D. The plan line's prose is the best-written thing in the game.
3. That is also the problem: following the lit button is the whole game and it beats par. Nine variants that deviated from the line were measured; the best gained 1%, the rest lost 1 to 8%. There is no skill ceiling above "obey", and the second daily is the first again.
4. The five levers, the hydro wheel, the tie knob and the Live Stack drag are never asked for and are a trap: one touch halts the dispatch until RE-DISPATCH, which discards the touch. The "desk as instrument" pillar is a display the player must not touch. This is Q-18 by design, and the spec's own L-6 says the opposite.
5. The first minute is the worst minute: a 250-word briefing, 81 controls on screen one, two clock holds at about 22 and 28 real seconds for feeder cards whose only sensible answer is Enter, and an Esc that silently leaves 240 MW dark at $30,000 a megawatt-hour.
6. The signature moment misfires: the beat strip lights GOVERNORS first and never BATTERY on the evening trip whenever any governor or inverter MW precedes the battery (a one-line bug); on about a third of days the one full slow-motion watch is spent on a midday rise; after a relay trip the clock runs at 150x with 2,000 MW dark, while a 240 MW hand-back gets a hold.
7. The north star's third verb has no surface. The day ends on a regulator's worksheet: a dollar table, SAIDI, a hash, DOWNLOAD REPLAY LOG. No share card, no puzzle number, no streak, no link preview, no phone landing page, no closing line, no "moment".
8. Scoring reads as money and drifts: the same 150 MWh mistake is a B on a heatwave day and a C on a calm one, because the inert carbon term is half of par's total; the live SCORE chip said 1001 eleven minutes before directed shedding began.
9. The spec describes a build three slices old. About 40 claims are stale, nine accepts were quietly waived, its own differentiation rule ("the respond card is the only hold") has been overruled twice, and "in five minutes" is really six and a half to seven on a first shift.
10. Nobody but the owner has ever played. Every stranger-facing accept and both playtest gates are unmeasured. The cheapest next step is a greybox check with two people, after the S-sized first-minute fixes below.

## 2. What is strong (keep it)

- `sim/`: no `Date`, `Math.random`, timers or transcendentals per tick (enforced by `tests/sim-lint.test.js`); `observe()` copies keys so the desk cannot reach live state; replay throws on a refused input; one input log reproduces a day at any speed.
- The realism ledger. `sim/params.js` wraps every number with its source or a "simplified" label; `content/text.js` builds the "?" sentences from the params so words cannot drift from numbers. FOS bands, mandatory PFR, battery droop, UFLS, AS/NZS 4777.2 inverters, VCR, VER and STPIS exclusions are right.
- The plan line's prose: "Start CCGT 2 now: it takes 49 min to reach the grid", "Stop it: saves about $45,000 if nothing trips before then", "it spins for free and adds spare". The `? IF PRESSED` consequence line before a guarded press.
- The BALANCE bar in words ("SHORT 170 MW held up by battery 140 · governors 30"), the feeder card's RESTORE / GO ON faces, the tray's "TO THE ..." buttons, the night strip counting up.
- The watch's physics and its first-time captions ("YOUR GUARD: 400 MW. The battery answers within a second."); the respond card's "Caught by" chain; the over-frequency watch showing inverters backing off (rare in any public explainer).
- Par: information-fair (S-4), re-plans like the player, gates 365 dailies so every daily is solvable. The suburb personas ("On off-peak hot water since 1962; has never asked why.").
- Test tooling: `tools/play.mjs` jumps a day in seconds; `npm run test:changed`.

## 4. Design weaknesses (built as intended; hurts fun or clarity)

### 4.1 Follow the line is the whole game (agency, stranger, fun-design lenses)

Evidence: a scripted player that does exactly what the line says, once a grid-minute, scores 1004 A★ on 20261014 (24 asks) and 1044 A★ on the weekend (23 asks); a stranger-shaped version that only clicks START guards scores 975 A. Nine deviations the sim supports but the line never offers: withholding Act I's spare asks +8 points (nothing can trip before 09:00: `content/scenarios.js:235-255`), lowering the GUARD at the peak +6 on the desk day but −80 with $652k of UFLS on the weekend, no stops −46 (958, B), a coal stop in the belly −36, one soak instead of six −10. The reward surface is flat around par's policy and the line sits on top. Of 24 asks, 6 are identical free soak bookings, 2 are feeder cards with one right answer, 9 are starts where the line names the unit, 3 stops, 3 battery orders; two reasonable players would differ on perhaps 4–5. Par is the line's rule set (`app/objective.js` mirrors `PAR_*` constants and imports `aimFlex` from `sim/autopilot.js`).

The one deep decision the sim has, GUARD versus energy on the battery, is hidden by branch order: `spare` is read before `battery`, so a player who lowers the ring to free the dial sees "Raise the battery GUARD to 400 MW" until they put it back; every discharge the line sizes is "all the GUARD leaves free". Par itself steps the ring 400 → 100 between 18:01 and 18:50.

The trip clock flattens it further: Act III is a trip of the largest synchronised machine in 17:00–19:30 on about 75% of days, so spare at the peak is a certainty to prepare for, not a risk to weigh. The one lever the player has over the moment (keep the largest unit small) is never told.

Proposals (owner's call, interview Q1): keep the prescriptive line on the first shift; afterwards state the problem and light the candidates; price both sides of the stops, the spare and the GUARD in the line ("GT·A for the next hour: about $50k; a 620 MW trip uncaught: about $2M at VCR"); say in the briefing that the peak gets a trip. Realism: operators get LOR notices, not orders; FCAS-vs-energy is co-optimised every five minutes.

### 4.2 The desk is a display with traps (desk-ui, agency)

81 enabled controls at the briefing; 28 START/STOP guards whose faces at rest are glyphs (`○2`, `■`, `·`); the day's most-asked control (GUARD, 5 objective rules target it) is an unlabelled 68-px ring with no hover text; the levers, hydro wheel, tie knob and stack drag are never asked for and a touch sets `sys.edited` (`app/system.js:14-16, 52-56`) so the 5-minute dispatch stops for every lever until RE-DISPATCH, which discards the edit (one measured touch at 06:00 produced "‼ URGENT With levers held by hand the plan is 140 MW below demand now"). Sceptic's corrections: the battery dial and ring jump on click but are re-dispatched over, only the tie holds the levers; the hold is Q-18's recorded design, so it is a conflict with L-6/K-1/K-2, not a defect. Twelve 8.5-px alarm tiles carry acronyms (HIGH RoCoF, UFLS OPERATED, AGC LIMIT, MIN GEN). The header "?" opens the realism ledger, not how-to; the controls how-to lives only on the briefing card and cannot be re-read; seven "?" badges sit in a 32-px header. The Live Stack has become a chart with a secret drag; "L" needs two presses.

Proposal (interview Q2): the commitment desk. Levers, wheel and stack become motorised readouts (keep the glide, the band, the cone, the gate); per station a lamp row plus two word buttons ▶ START / ■ STOP (28 → 12); GUARD as a labelled slider with a "SET 400" chip when the line asks; the tie knob stays but no longer holds the levers; RE-DISPATCH leaves the AGC desk; plain tile words (LOW FREQ, NOT SECURE, FAST FALL, LOAD SHED, NO ROOM, SPILLING); the header "?" opens HOW TO PLAY first. HAND mode keeps live levers as its harder game. Cost M. Realism improves (NEMDE sets base points; operators commit, reserve, tie, restore).

### 4.3 The first minute (stranger, fun-design, trip-moment)

Measured on 20261014, first shift: START at 0 s (clock held, good); GUARD at 20.6 s on an unlabelled ring; soak ask at 27.8 s; feeder card at 22.2 s holds the clock; a second feeder card about 6 s later; six inputs on four control types and two holds in 37 s. Then 90 s of near-nothing in the belly with a locked 29-s watch in the middle, then a pleasant 60-s beat of starts, then 40 s of STEADY. The feeder card's "Esc: go on" leaves the feeder dark and yours: ALL-IN 9.0c → 21.7c (07:15) → 115.2c (09:15); on the do-nothing day 64% of the dark MWh (5,907 of 9,229) are the two feeders left after Esc, relit by the night shift at 22:20. The card does say "it stays dark and is yours" but never prices it. The taught restore is answered by an UNDER FREQ tile and "BAND LOW 5 MIN" (cold-load dip to 49.77 Hz; P2, no horn) with nothing saying "that is the cold load; it recovers". When the card holds while R would be refused (frequency 0.023 Hz under the permissive), Esc removes the hold for good (`app/director.js:349`), three grid-seconds before R would have worked (the owner's open question in desk/README §32.11).

The briefing is about 250 words (48 role + 57 goals + 103 keys + day/watch + seed line) against D-3's 60; it opens the goals paragraph with the VCR/VER price list; the seed line is developer text.

Proposals (interview Q5, Q6): one card for both feeders ("RESTORE HAR4 now; HAR3 at 07:15" queued), or one hold per day; Esc restores when allowed and otherwise asks "Leave 240 MW dark? About $7M an hour"; a card holds only when R will be accepted; no UNDER FREQ chime for five minutes after a permissive restore; one "soak the city" booking; the soak ask waits for the belly; a 60-word briefing with the keys behind "?"; hide HAND on a first shift.

### 4.4 The signature moment (trip-moment, stranger, fun-design)

- D1 above (the beat order) is the biggest single fix.
- `app/director.js:101` gives the full 29-s watch to the first contingency of any kind; on today's daily that is a tie export trip at 12:19 ("Load is lost ... Frequency rises", peak 50.196 Hz, "N-1 SECURE"); the 19:19 fall that the player's GUARD catches then plays compact at 0.5x with word-only beats. About a third of days open with a rise (20–35% of belly trips; 30–60% on heat days). "YOUR GUARD" is said only in the first full watch; the card folds the guard into "battery 494 MW".
- RESPOND's 30x never runs: on six seeds the clock report says `RESPOND 0x`, because frequency is back in band inside the watch. After Enter on a relay trip the clock goes straight to 150x with "9 districts dark (2146 MW): relight them and cover 700 MW within 29:30" (impossible as stated: restores are 5 min apart). Each real second reading costs about 90 MWh, $2.7M at VCR. Q-91's own reasoning (15 s late on a 240 MW feeder cost a C) argues far more strongly for a hold here.
- After the nadir (T+1.8–2.4 s) the 0.15x segment runs to T+3 and 1x to T+10 with the caption template unchanged: about 15 of 29 real seconds are after the drama.
- About a third of days have no evening trip (heat 15% has none by `content/scenarios.js` act3; storm without a link trip about 18%); about 7% have no trip at all. An under-planned evening goes dark one district a grid-minute at 150x with a tray card and no watch, which is the physics a failing stranger most needs to see.
- RESTORE's 2-s FOCUS ends before the pickup's dip bottoms; the mini-skill is a formality. SYNC was demoted (Q-89) because a clean close never changes an outcome.

Proposals (interview Q4, Q6): full watch for the first falling trip ≥300 MW (per kind: `seen.fall`/`seen.rise`); an adaptive schedule read off the record (0.15x to the extreme + 0.5 s, then 1x to T+10); a compact watch on the first directed shed; after a relay trip either RESPOND at 30x until the first input or dark districts hold the clock by UFLS stage as the morning feeders do; a longer FOCUS with a pinned pickup nadir ("240 MW of cold load picked up: battery 100, governors 60, bottom 49.74 Hz") and "clean pickups" counted; retire the scope from AGC days or re-aim it at the tripped unit's return.

### 4.5 The ending and the score (scoring-daily, comparables, fun-design)

- The end card (`app/endcard.js:72-150`): SCORE n · L, a YOU/PAR/Δ dollar table with "× $30,000 (VCR)" and "× $80 (VER)" arithmetic, "Biggest gap to par: SUPPLY +$198k (more or dearer plant than par's)" naming no plant and no hour, SAIDI/SAIFI/MAIFI, STPIS, seed, hash, DOWNLOAD REPLAY LOG, PLAY THIS DAY AGAIN. On the D day nothing says "you went on past two feeder cards and left 5,907 MWh dark for fifteen hours" though `sim/market.js` `splitShed` already keeps uflsMWh / directedMWh / taskMWh / taskLateMWh.
- Letters drift: points = 1000 × par ÷ you is a hyperbola in the dollar gap, so the same +150 MWh dark is 766 (B) on the 20261016 heatwave and 653 (C) on 20261014; +600 MWh is a C there and a D here. Cause: the carbon term is 52.7% of par's total on a calm day and the player cannot move it (the follower's whole day moved it 0.4%).
- The live SCORE chip read 1001 at 18:19 on the Enter-only day, 10 minutes before directed shedding, and 270 two seconds after the trip; on the A day it fell 981 → 919 with nothing wrong because par's belly timetable differs.
- Nothing on any screen says the day is the same for everyone: the briefing shows "Seed 20261014".
- Y-2's "one official attempt" (unbuilt) would stamp a stranger's learning run as the card; PLAY THIS DAY AGAIN already replays instantly with par cached.

Proposals (interview Q3, Q7, Q8): the dark-MWh split by cause on the card (S, no sim change); letters on MWh-equivalent gaps (gap ÷ VCR: A ≤10, B ≤150, C ≤600) so a letter means the same on every day, with rank names (TRAINEE … SHIFT SUPERVISOR … MARG'S CHAIR); the SCORE chip retired until 21:00 or relabelled; a share card from `score.spark` (already recorded per 2-h block) in Y-4's format with a puzzle number, date, closing line and COPY; a static ladder per date from the proxies daily-gen already runs ("Did nothing 11 · Pressed Enter 54 · Followed the line 975 · YOU · PAR 1000 · Competent 1012"); "the moment" and "what if" as a decision diff against par (par's input log is dropped when it finishes: keeping it is one line) instead of D-22's 30 re-simulations.

### 4.6 Realism: two invented rules and one unlabelled hero (realism-edu)

- The GUARD ring, the game's one signature decision, has no "?" row, no §8.2 row and no face text saying it is contingency FCAS set by hand (`content/anchors.js` has no `ring-guard`). "Big batteries make much of their money holding power back to catch trips" is the most repeatable NEM fact in the game and nobody is told it.
- H-2's hot-unit trip (D11): an invented rule, unlabelled, armed by governor duty.
- The directed-shedding timers (1 min under 49.5 Hz, 5 min outside the band, one district a minute) are taught in the UNDER FREQ explainer as part of the frequency standard ("All well established"); they are GRIDWATCH's own rule standing in for AEMO directing the networks. Keep the drama; tag the rule.
- TIGHT can mean "the next trip sheds two suburbs" (05:30 no-input: TIGHT with a preview of 48.99 Hz, below UFLS stage 1). A fifth head word, TRIP SHEDS, when the preview is below 49.0 Hz.
- A hydro machine on line at 0 MW gives inertia and governor response for no water: the inertia lesson the modern NEM answers with synchronous condensers and grid-forming batteries, unnamed.
- Myth a day leaves behind: "if the control room does nothing the lights go out at 18:30" (in the real NEM generators self-commit for the price). One briefing sentence fixes it: "The desk hands you every decision the market and the operator make between them."
- Nothing here needs physics bent. Where realism costs fun (auto-sync at 4 min killed SYNC; RERT at $16k and ≤25% days is a story most strangers never see; coal's 8-h minimum down time makes "cycle coal" a non-decision) the owner can now choose (interview Q11).

### 4.7 Variety and voice (comparables, fun-design)

Every daily has the same skeleton: two feeders 06:50–07:50, one belly event, one evening trip, the same fleet; what varies is which of four crises and its hour (§3.3's measured 0.79 correlation between calm days stands; seasons are in Phase 4 "on the cut list"). Marg appears zero times in the build; the only character lines are the six suburb wits. The best 15 seconds (the watch captions) come at minute four, only for a player still there; the link has no image. Every future date's class is already computable from its seed (`sim/dayplan.js:16`), so a "This week: Tue mild, Wed storm, Thu HEATWAVE" line costs a loop. Real NEM incidents (Callide 2021, SA 2016, the Victorian towers 2024, Loy Yang A3 2017) are five unbuilt debrief footnotes; the director's scenario data could carry them as labelled anniversary dailies, with SA 2016 as the practice shelf's first Hard day.

## 8. The interview

Fifteen decisions, ordered by how much the answer changes the game. The recommended option is first in each list. Ask them four at a time.

**Q1 · Agency.** The plan line names the exact unit, MW and minute, and following it beats par; nine measured deviations gain at most 1%. What should the line be after the first shift?
- A (recommended): prescriptive on the first shift; from the second daily (or a COACH toggle) it states the problem and lights the candidates, with a "no coach" tag on the share card. Keeps strangers safe, gives returning players a game.
- B: keep it prescriptive always; the game is execution and the trades are the levers. Simplest; no skill ceiling above obedience.
- C: keep it prescriptive but price both sides of every ask (stops, spare, GUARD vs energy) so the player can disagree with a number. Cheapest; the line stays the plan.
- D: A and C together.

**Q2 · The desk.** Levers, wheel, knob and stack drag are never asked for and a touch halts the dispatch until RE-DISPATCH (Q-18). Which desk?
- A (recommended): the commitment desk: levers, wheel and stack become motorised readouts; per-station START/STOP word buttons with machine lamps; GUARD a labelled slider; the tie knob stays but never holds the levers; HAND keeps live levers. About 81 → 55 controls, no glyph-only faces (M).
- B: honour L-6: a lever or knob move becomes a plan keyframe the re-flow keeps, and the line may ask "pull GT·A to 300 by 07:45" (M, keeps the fantasy literal).
- C: faces only: keep all 81 controls, fix the 28 glyph guards, the GUARD label and the header "?" (S, keeps the trap).
- D: A plus a quiet plan bar: twelve tiles collapse to one ALARMS lamp with a count (M).

**Q3 · Share first.** The north star's third verb has no code: no share card, puzzle number, streak, link preview or phone page. Build the share loop before 2d and 2e?
- A (recommended): yes: a thin share slice now (text card from `score.spark`, COPY, "#N · date · same day for everyone", closing lines, og tags, phone landing), then the debrief.
- B: as planned: 2d (debrief) → 2e (onboarding) → Phase 4 (share).
- C: 2e minimal first, then the share slice, then 2d.

**Q4 · After the big trip.** After Enter on a relay trip the clock runs at 150x with 2,000 MW dark; RESPOND's 30x never runs. What should hold?
- A (recommended): dark districts hold the clock at each allowed second by UFLS stage, as the morning feeder cards do (M).
- B: RESPOND runs at 30x from the card until the first restore or START, capped (S).
- C: as built: the card lights the controls and the clock runs.

**Q5 · The morning holds.** Two feeder cards hold the clock at about 22 and 28 real seconds, each with one sensible answer; Esc leaves 240 MW dark and yours. Which?
- A (recommended): one card for both hand-backs ("RESTORE HAR4 now; HAR3 at 07:15" queued), Esc restores when allowed or asks a priced "Leave it dark?", and a card holds only when R would be accepted.

- D: keep Q-91 as built.

**Q6 · The slow motion.** The once-only full watch goes to the first contingency of any kind, which is a midday rise on about a third of days; the evening fall the GUARD catches plays compact. Also: should a directed shed get a watch?

- C: keep as built.

**Q7 · Scoring.** Letters drift with the day (150 MWh = B on a heatwave, C on a calm day) because the inert carbon term is half of par's total. Which fix?
- A (recommended): keep points; define letters on MWh-equivalent gaps (gap ÷ VCR: A ≤10, B ≤150, C ≤600, D above, F black); say so on the card; carbon stays in the number.

**Q8 · The official attempt.** Y-2 (unbuilt) makes the first run past 09:00 official and later runs practice; a stranger's first run is a learning run.
